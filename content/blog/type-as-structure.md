Letterforms are not decoration. They are the load-bearing walls of communication.

This is not a metaphor. When a typeface fails, the message fails. When the hierarchy is unclear, the reader does the designer's job for them — and resents it.

## what a typeface carries

A typeface carries weight before it carries words. The reader feels Helvetica before they read it. They feel Garamond. These are not neutral vessels.

The Bauhaus understood this. Herbert Bayer's *Universal* typeface was an attempt to strip the letterform to pure function — no serifs, no ornamentation, just the minimum geometry required for legibility.

It was not a great typeface. But it was an honest experiment.

## hierarchy as argument

Every piece of text makes an argument about what matters most. The title says: *start here*. The subhead says: *this is how it subdivides*. The body says: *here is the detail*.

When designers fail to establish hierarchy, they are not being neutral. They are refusing to make an argument. And that refusal is itself a statement — usually an unintentional one.

**Make the argument. Own it.**
